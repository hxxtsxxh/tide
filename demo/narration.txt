Research answers can outlive the facts they depend on. Ripple keeps the connection between a source, a quoted fact and a decision.

I create a board with my own question and two public Telem documentation pages. Telem reads those selected pages to qualify them, and direct Tavily Extract returns the page text that Ripple uses. Each fact must quote text that was actually returned, and the board shows the source, the extractor and the provider request ID.

Dependencies are proposed by the model. They are for review, not proof that every relationship has been found.

Next I change the requirement text from three to eight. That is my input, not a change on the web, so Ripple does not read the sources again. It recomputes only the answers that depend on the changed requirement and reuses the rest. The counts on screen are the ones this recording observes.

Last, I check the evidence. Ripple rechecks the selected pages with Telem and direct Tavily. If a page has not changed, its answers are reused. A failed or partial read leaves the dependent answers unknown rather than fresh.

The Simulation Lab is not part of this recording. It is a labeled synthetic teaching tool that runs only in the browser.

Figures from earlier local runs are recorded in the verification files and are not measured for this recording. Savings from refresh are not measured. Two services reading one URL count as one source. The deployed HTTPS gates and native client checks are separate and are not shown here.
